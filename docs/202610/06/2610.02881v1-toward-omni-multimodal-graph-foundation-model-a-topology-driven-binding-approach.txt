ABSTRACT
Multimodal graph foundation models (MGFMs) seek to learn generalizable rep-
resentations from large-scale graphs with heterogeneous node modalities. How-
ever, real-world Multimodal-Attributed Graphs (MAGs) often contain incomplete
node attributes, limiting the scale and diversity of available pretraining corpora.
Besides, existing MGFMs primarily incorporate graph topology as structural con-
text, overlooking its role in guiding multimodal binding and shaping a unified
representation space. To address these challenges, we propose GraphBind, a
topology-driven approach that uses graph topology to bind rich modality infor-
mation into a unified shared space. GraphBind is motivated by the stability of
graph topology, which provides structural references and complementary seman-
tic information for multimodal binding. Concretely, GraphBind uses topology to
organize self semantics and reliable neighborhood semantics into a global shared
space that integrates structure and semantics, and adapts this space to discrimina-
tive and generative tasks through lightweight interfaces. Extensive experiments
against 11 representative baselines demonstrate that GraphBind achieves leading
performance on both discriminative and generative tasks, with relative improve-
ments of up to 28.1% over the strongest baseline.

INTRODUCTION
Graph foundation models (GFMs) aim to learn generalizable graph representations through large-
scale pretraining across graph domains and downstream tasks Liu et al. (2023a); Wang et al. (2025a),
showing practical value in cross-domain graph classification and graph instruction tuning Liu et al.
(2024); Tang et al. (2024). As real-world graphs increasingly contain heterogeneous modalities
such as text and images Zhu et al. (2025); Wan et al. (2026), multimodal graph foundation mod-
els (MGFMs) have attracted growing attention in recent years. By integrating graph topology with
multimodal attributes, MGFMs learn transferable representations for diverse graph tasks while sup-
porting multimodal reasoning and generation He et al. (2025); Liu et al. (2026); Sun et al. (2026).
Despite substantial progress, learning reliable shared representations from graphs with incomplete
node modalities remains challenging, which creates a critical data bottleneck for large-scale MAG
pretraining. This bottleneck can be summarized from three perspectives: (1) Incomplete Pretrain-
ing Corpora. Real-world MAGs often contain incomplete node attributes. Restricting pretraining
to nodes with complete observations substantially limits the scale and diversity of usable graph cor-
pora Wu et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2023). (2) Fragmented Semantic Spaces. Separate modality
encoding and alignment may produce fragmented semantic spaces, making it difficult to construct
the unified representation space required by MGFMs. (3) Limited Topological Binding. Exist-
ing MGFMs primarily incorporate graph topology as structural context, with limited exploration of
its role in guiding multimodal binding and shaping a unified representation space He et al. (2025);
Liu et al. (2026); Sun et al. (2026). Our empirical study in Sec. 3 shows that graph neighborhoods
provide additional semantic signals beyond node-local modalities. This finding supports the use of
topology as a stable reference when learning from pretraining corpora with incomplete modalities.

Building upon this observation, we propose GraphBind, a novel MGFM framework that binds het-
erogeneous modality semantics through graph topology into a unified representation space. The
key insight of GraphBind is that graph topology provides a stable structural reference and useful
neighborhood information for multimodal binding. Concretely, GraphBind consists of three coupled
designs. (1) Incomplete Corpus Pretraining. GraphBind explicitly introduces modality absence
into pretraining, enabling the model to fully utilize incomplete MAGs as pretraining corpora and im-
prove its robustness. (2) Topology-Driven Multimodal Binding. GraphBind uses graph topology
as structural guidance to organize node semantics, neighborhood context, and multimodal attributes
into a unified representation space. (3) Unified Adaptation. GraphBind adapts the unified repre-
sentation space to both discriminative and generative tasks through lightweight fine-tuning while
preserving the shared semantic interface learned during pretraining.
Our Contributions: (1) In-depth Investigation. We revisit realistic modality absence in MAGs
and empirically show that graph neighborhoods provide useful recovery signals beyond the avail-
able attributes of each node. (2) Novel Framework. We propose GraphBind, a topology-guided
multimodal graph foundation model that integrates missing-modality pretraining, topology-driven
semantic recovery, and unified representation adaptation. (3) SOTA Performance. Extensive exper-
iments against eleven representative baselines demonstrate leading performance on discriminative
and generative tasks, with relative improvements of up to 5.4% in MRR and 28.1% in BLEU-4,
respectively.

2.2
GRAPH FOUNDATION MODELS
Graph foundation models learn transferable representations through pretraining across graph do-
mains and tasks Liu et al. (2023a); Wang et al. (2025a). Existing methods represent graph compu-
tation with task trees or transferable vocabularies Wang et al. (2025b; 2024), learn reusable graph
tokens Xia et al. (2024), or model graphs in geometric spaces Sun et al. (2025). These methods
support broad transfer across graph tasks, while their pretraining mainly focuses on graph structure
and a single attribute space. MAGs additionally require a shared space that connects heterogeneous
node attributes with topology.
2.3
MULTIMODAL GRAPH LEARNING
Multimodal graph learning combines graph topology with node attributes such as text and im-
ages Zhu et al. (2025); Wan et al. (2026). Earlier methods use graph convolution, attention, or
global graph structures to fuse modalities for specific tasks Wei et al. (2019); Tao et al. (2020);
Guo et al. (2024), while recent methods jointly update topology and modality representations or as-
sign different roles to neighbors Zhu et al. (2026); Zuo et al. (2026). Multimodal graph foundation
models further learn shared representations for transfer across datasets and tasks He et al. (2025);
Liu et al. (2026). Together, these studies explore how node attributes and graph relations can be
jointly modeled to support prediction within individual graphs and transfer across graph domains
and tasks.

EMPIRICAL INVESTIGATION
In this section, we empirically examine why graph topology can serve as a structural reference for
multimodal graph foundation modeling. We focus on two research questions: Q1: Are node-local
modalities sufficient for multimodal binding? Q2: Can graph topology support modality recovery
when neither modality of the center node is available?
Datasets, Methods, and Tasks. We conduct two recovery studies to analyze the semantic roles
of node-local modalities and graph topology. We compare GraphBind with MMGCN, UniGraph2,
and MGAT on modality feature recovery. Recovery quality is measured by the cosine similarity
between the predicted feature and the corresponding reference feature. The first study is conducted
on Grocery under single-modality absence, where either the textual or visual modality of the cen-
ter node is unavailable. We compare two recovery settings with identical target-modality masks.
Single-Modality (SM) recovery uses only the center node’s remaining modality, with neighborhood
messages and topology inputs disabled. Graph-Assisted (GA) recovery uses available features from
one-hop neighbors. Neither setting receives the center node’s target modality. The second study is
conducted on RedditS when both modalities are absent, where both textual and visual modalities of
the center node are unavailable. We use a feature-mean prototype as the non-structural reference
and evaluate GA recovery for textual and visual semantics from surrounding nodes.
Individual node modalities provide limited semantic information (Answer for Q1). We first
examine whether the remaining modality of a node provides sufficient information for semantic
recovery. As shown in Fig. 1, SM consistently achieves lower recovery similarity than GA across all
four methods under both absence settings. This observation indicates that the remaining modality
provides useful but limited binding information. The limitation is especially clear when textual
semantics need to be inferred from visual attributes, where the available visual information only
partially reflects the missing textual semantics. Therefore, combining available node attributes with
neighborhood information supports more effective multimodal binding in MAGs.
Graph topology provides shared semantic information (Answer for Q2). We further examine
whether topology can provide semantic information when the center node has no available modality.
As shown in Fig. 2, all graph-assisted methods achieve higher recovery similarity than the feature-
mean prototype for both text and vision when neither modality of the center node is available. This
result shows that graph neighborhoods preserve semantic information that is shared across nodes
and transferable across target modalities. This observation suggests that graph topology can act as a
structural reference for organizing surrounding node semantics in MAGs.
The two observations above motivate a topology-driven approach to multimodal graph foundation
modeling. The available modalities provide useful but limited information for recovering miss-
ing modalities, as different modalities capture different aspects of node semantics. In comparison,
graph topology provides a structural context for integrating multimodal information across con-
nected nodes. These findings suggest that graph topology can serve as a structural reference for
integrating multimodal information across nodes into shared semantic representations. This sup-
ports MGFM pretraining on graph corpora with incomplete node modalities.

METHODOLOGY
In this section, we present GraphBind, a multimodal graph foundation model that uses graph topol-
ogy as a structural reference to bind node modalities into a unified representation space. As il-
lustrated in Fig. 3, we first simulate realistic modality absence during pretraining, then construct
a topology-guided shared representation through self-semantic initialization, reliable neighborhood
aggregation, and topology-guided latent calibration, and finally adapt the learned shared space to
discriminative and generative tasks with lightweight task interfaces.
4.1
MODALITY ABSENCE SIMULATION
Large-scale MAG pretraining cannot rely only on nodes with complete modalities, since naturally
collected graph corpora have uneven modality availability. GraphBind therefore controls modality
availability for each node, allowing incomplete MAGs to participate in pretraining.

cally, we consider three simulation protocols: Independent Absence randomly removes each modal-
ity. Neighborhood Correlated Absence removes modalities from adjacent nodes to represent local
acquisition failure. Property Dependent Absence sets the absence probability according to node
properties such as degree or community membership. The input availability received by the encoder

4.3
UNIFIED DOWNSTREAM ADAPTATION

geometry. For discriminative tasks, we design a topology prefix prompter to perform task-specific

into continuous token embeddings for language and image generators.

EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we first describe the experimental setup. Then, we conduct comprehensive eval-
uations to answer the following research questions: Q1: How does GraphBind perform against
representative methods on discriminative and generative tasks? Q2: What are the contributions of
the main components in GraphBind? Q3: How robust is GraphBind under varying modality absence
patterns? Q4: What are the computational and storage costs of GraphBind?
5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Datasets.
We evaluate GraphBind on eight representative MAG datasets from the OpenMAG
benchmark Wan et al. (2026) covering e-commerce, social media, video, image-text, and art do-
mains. Specifically, Grocery, Movies, and Toys Ni et al. (2019), together with RedditS Desai et al.
(2021), are used for node classification; DY and Bili Dance Zhang et al. (2024) for link predic-
tion; Flickr30k Plummer et al. (2015) for graph-to-text generation; and SemArt Garcia & Vogiatzis
(2018) for graph-to-image generation. Detailed dataset statistics are provided in the Appendix A.4.
Baselines. We consider three categories of representative methods: (1) MAG models: MMGCN Wei
et al. (2019), MGAT Tao et al. (2020), LGMRec Guo et al. (2024), TMTE Zhu et al. (2026), and
RoleMAG Zuo et al. (2026); (2) GFMs: GIT Wang et al. (2025b), GFT Wang et al. (2024), Open-
Graph Xia et al. (2024), and RiemannGFM Sun et al. (2025); and (3) MGFMs: UniGraph2 He et al.
(2025) and PLANET Liu et al. (2026). Detailed descriptions and implementation settings for all
baselines are provided in the Appendix A.5.
Downstream Tasks. We evaluate GraphBind on two task families: (1) Discriminative tasks, in-
cluding node classification and link prediction; and (2) Generative tasks, including graph-to-text
generation (G2Text) and graph-to-image generation (G2Image). Detailed task protocols and evalu-
ation metrics are provided in the Appendix A.6.
5.2
MAIN RESULTS (ANSWER FOR Q1)

5.3
ABLATION STUDY (ANSWER FOR Q2)
To address Q2, we compare GraphBind with 5 variants to examine its core architecture, which
couples reliable neighborhood semantic completion with topology guided shared latent calibration.
Since these stages are coupled, each variant replaces them together under the same experimental pro-
tocol. We organize the variants into three groups: (1) Simple Propagation: We apply standard GNN
encoders GCN Kipf & Welling (2017), GAT Veliˇckovi´c et al. (2018), and GraphSAGE Hamilton
et al. (2017) over the original adjacency, retaining neighborhood propagation without neighbor reli-
ability estimation, the self retention gate, or topology conditioned calibration; (2) Topology Encod-
ing (T.E.): We apply a node-wise semantic transformation using an MLP with both node semantics
and topology encodings, without neighborhood propagation; and (3) Node Only (N.O.): We remove
topology encodings and the entire graph aware path, directly using the initialized node semantics.
As shown in Table 3, GraphBind achieves the best result on all four tasks, with larger gains over the
strongest variants on the two generative tasks. Among the variants, Simple Propagation performs
better on Toys and DY, while N.O. performs best on SemArt and Flickr30k. This task-dependent or-
dering indicates that direct propagation over the original adjacency provides useful information for
discriminative graph tasks and can introduce unsuitable context for generation. Moreover, T.E. per-
forms below N.O. on every dataset, showing that adding a node-wise transformation and topology
encoding without neighborhood propagation does not improve upon self semantics alone. Over-
all, the proposed topology-guided encoder outperforms the tested alternatives across all four tasks,
supporting its effectiveness in learning shared graph representations.
5.4
ROBUSTNESS ANALYSIS (ANSWER FOR Q3)
To answer Q3, we evaluate robustness to modality absence on Grocery and RedditS by masking
modality inputs at inference. Specifically, we impose two noise patterns to model distinct failure
processes. Independent noise masks each node’s modalities separately and simulates dispersed ran-
dom failures. Neighborhood noise masks locally concentrated nodes and simulates correlated fail-
ures within a community or collection context. We increase the missing ratio from 0.0 to 0.8 in steps
of 0.1 and report macro-F1. As shown in Fig. 4, performance declines for all methods as modal-
ity absence increases. Additionally, GraphBind exhibits the smoothest degradation and remains the
leading method across both datasets and missing patterns. Under neighborhood noise, GraphBind
retains leading performance even when correlated modality failures remove locally shared informa-
tion, showing that it remains effective when modality absence is concentrated in neighboring nodes.
Together, the results support the robustness of GraphBind to increasing modality absence under
noisy settings.

EFFICIENCY ANALYSIS (ANSWER FOR Q4)
To answer Q4, we compare the computational costs of GraphBind with representative baselines in
Table 4. GraphBind’s finetuning and inference costs are higher than those of most lightweight MAG
baselines, whereas its pretraining is faster than that of both MGFMs and its inference is faster than
PLANET; this stage-dependent pattern indicates that GraphBind reduces repeated pretraining cost
while incurring additional downstream cost for processing richer graph context. Moreover, among
MGFMs, GraphBind requires substantially fewer trainable parameters, updating 5.32M parameters
compared with 20.72M for UniGraph2 and 221.94M for PLANET, respectively. It also achieves the
lowest proportion of trainable parameters among all compared methods. Combined with its strong
classification performance, these findings demonstrate the effectiveness and parameter efficiency of
GraphBind’s lightweight finetuning design.
6
CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose GraphBind, a multimodal graph foundation model that uses graph topol-
ogy to bind heterogeneous modality semantics into a unified representation space. We revisit the
role of topology in multimodal binding and show that existing methods largely treat it as a propa-
gation or alignment signal, underexploiting its capacity to organize shared semantics. GraphBind
couples topology-guided pretraining with lightweight fine-tuning, learning a shared space that inte-
grates structure and semantics for both discriminative and generative tasks. Extensive experiments
against 11 representative baselines show that GraphBind achieves strong performance across both
discriminative and generative tasks, demonstrating strong generalization and robustness. We believe
this work provides a valuable foundation for topology-driven multimodal graph foundation models
and motivates further exploration of scalable graph-semantic binding.

i

G2M-Adapter. For Eq. 8, the context sequence places the target node first, followed by its incoming
one-hop neighbors from the task metadata. Repeated entries and the target itself are removed from
the neighbor list, which is truncated in metadata order. Unused slots contain zero vectors and are
masked as attention keys.
For text generation, Fs projects the shared representations and applies local message passing with
bidirectional links from the target to its neighbors and between consecutive retained neighbors.
Each block combines transformed self states and mean neighbor states through GELU, a residual
connection, and LayerNorm. Slot embeddings distinguish context positions. Learnable queries
aggregate these states through the gated attention in Eq. 8, and Os applies LayerNorm. The resulting
tokens are prepended to the language input embeddings, with prefix length determined by the query
bank.
For image generation, Fs is a linear projection and Qs = Es gives self-attention over context nodes.
After the gated residual update, Os adds slot embeddings, normalizes and projects the states, and
zeros invalid slots. A final linear projection expands each slot into generator tokens followed by
LayerNorm, retaining the padded slots in the output sequence. These tokens are concatenated with
text embeddings and supplied to the diffusion U-Net through its existing cross-attention interface.

Grocery, Movies, and Toys. These datasets are extracted from the Amazon product review cor-
pus Ni et al. (2019). Nodes represent products, and edges encode purchase relations derived from
the also bought metadata. Textual attributes include product titles, descriptions, and reviews, while
visual attributes are product images. Grocery and Toys are used for node classification, and Movies
is used for few shot node classification. Labels are given by product categories or genres.
RedditS. RedditS is derived from the RedCaps collection of Reddit posts with images and text De-
sai et al. (2021). We follow the OpenMAG preprocessing protocol to construct its post graph Wan
et al. (2026). Post titles and bodies provide textual attributes, and embedded images provide vi-
sual attributes. We use RedditS for few shot node classification, where labels identify subreddit
communities.
DY and Bili Dance. These datasets are drawn from the NineRec benchmark Zhang et al. (2024).
Nodes represent short videos from Douyin and Bilibili, respectively, and edges reflect user co-
viewing or co-interaction behavior. Textual attributes are obtained from titles, descriptions, or hash-
tags, and visual attributes from video covers or sampled frames. Both datasets are used for link
prediction.
Ele-fashion. Ele-fashion is a heterogeneous Amazon graph that merges electronics and fashion
products Ni et al. (2019); Hou et al. (2026). Nodes represent products from the two categories, and
edges encode cross-category co-purchases. Textual attributes combine technical specifications with
style descriptions, while visual attributes are product images. We use Ele-fashion for cross-domain
pretraining.
Sports. Sports is drawn from Amazon’s Sports and Outdoors category Ni et al. (2019); Hou et al.
(2026). Nodes represent sports equipment and accessories, and edges capture complementary or
co-purchase relations. Textual attributes comprise product specifications, and visual attributes are
product images. We use Sports for cross-domain pretraining.
Flickr30k. Flickr30k Entities provides paired images and sentences with grounding annotations be-
tween regions and phrases Plummer et al. (2015). Following the OpenMAG construction Wan et al.
18


(2026), we link image regions and caption phrases through their grounding relations and evaluate
G2Text generation.
SemArt. SemArt is a fine-art collection introduced for multimodal retrieval and semantic art un-
derstanding Garcia & Vogiatzis (2018). Following the OpenMAG construction Wan et al. (2026),
nodes represent paintings and edges connect artworks sharing metadata such as artist, period, or
school. Textual attributes comprise titles and expert descriptions, while visual attributes are the
corresponding painting images. We use SemArt for G2Image generation.
A.5
COMPARED BASELINES
We compare GraphBind with representative methods from MAG models, GFMs, and MGFMs.
Mario Sun et al. (2026) focuses on adapting LLMs through modality-adaptive graph instruction
tuning, with downstream evaluation centered on node classification and link prediction. We leave
Mario outside the experimental comparison given its emphasis on LLM-based graph reasoning,
while our study mainly focuses on learning shared graph representations and adapting them to both
discriminative and generative tasks.
MMGCN Wei et al. (2019) is a multimodal graph convolution model for micro video recommen-
dation. It builds a separate user and item graph for each modality, propagates information indepen-
dently within each graph, and combines the resulting user and item representations with intrinsic
features and shared ID embeddings for personalized ranking.
MGAT Tao et al. (2020) is a multimodal graph attention model for recommendation. It applies gated
attention on parallel modality-specific interaction graphs, learns adaptive weights for neighboring
items and modalities, and fuses the resulting signals to capture fine-grained user preferences under
noisy or conflicting modalities.
LGMRec Guo et al. (2024) combines local graph learning with a global hypergraph module to
model collaborative and multimodal user interests for recommendation. Its local branch separates
collaborative and modality-specific signals, while the hypergraph branch captures higher-order de-
pendencies among users and items before the two representations are integrated.
TMTE Zhu et al. (2026) jointly and iteratively evolves graph topology and multimodal representa-
tions under downstream task supervision, forming a task-aware co-evolution framework for multi-
modal graph learning. It learns multi-perspective similarity relations to refine the adjacency struc-
ture and uses the evolved topology for smoothness-regularized multimodal fusion and cross-modal
alignment.
RoleMAG Zuo et al. (2026) routes neighbors according to learned roles and propagates multimodal
information through role-specific experts, targeting robust representation learning on multimodal
graphs. Its router decomposes each edge into shared, complementary, and heterophilous relations,
and the corresponding experts perform smoothing, cross-modal completion, and signed structural
filtering before gated fusion.
GIT Wang et al. (2025b) represents graph computation patterns as task trees and pretrains a shared
graph model through task-tree reconstruction. A task-tree representation unifies node-, edge-, and
graph-level objectives, after which the pretrained encoder supports supervised, few-shot, zero-shot,
and in-context adaptation across graph domains.
GFT Wang et al. (2024) learns a transferable vocabulary of computation trees with vector quanti-
zation and uses it to unify graph pretraining across domains and downstream fine tuning. During
pretraining, graph computation trees are reconstructed into a shared codebook. During adaptation,
the resulting tree tokens provide a common interface for node, link, and graph prediction.
OpenGraph Xia et al. (2024) distills graph generalization from large language models through
a unified graph tokenizer, a scalable graph Transformer, and LLM-assisted graph data augmenta-
tion. The tokenizer converts heterogeneous graph neighborhoods into transferable tokens, while the
Transformer models global node dependencies for zero-shot transfer across unseen graph domains
and tasks.
RiemannGFM Sun et al. (2025) formulates graph representation learning in a Riemannian geo-
metric space and pretrains a transferable graph foundation model for cross-domain node and link
19


prediction. Its geometric encoder and task-agnostic pretraining objective are designed to accommo-
date graphs with diverse structural properties while retaining transferable relational geometry.
UniGraph2 He et al. (2025) aligns text and image features with graph structure through frozen
modality encoders, a mixture-of-experts alignment module, and a universal GNN. The experts select
modality-specific alignment functions before topology-aware message passing, producing a unified
embedding space that transfers to node classification and link prediction.
PLANET Liu et al. (2026) is a multimodal graph foundation model based on a divide-and-conquer
design that separately models graph structure and modality semantics before integrating them for
transfer across discriminative and generative tasks. Its modular decomposition isolates graph encod-
ing, multimodal representation learning, and task adaptation, enabling a shared foundation model to
support heterogeneous downstream objectives.
A.6
EXPERIMENT SETUP
Experimental Environment. All experiments are implemented in Python 3.10.18 with PyTorch
2.8 and CUDA 12.9. We use a workstation equipped with Intel Xeon Scalable processors, 256 GB
system memory, and NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPUs with 48 GB VRAM each.
Pretraining Data and Sampling. We pretrain on Toys, Grocery, Movies, RedditS, Ele-fashion, and
Sports. Each dataset contributes one graph update per epoch, giving every dataset one sixth of the
updates. Ele-fashion and Sports use sampled subgraphs to control memory use. Textual and visual
node attributes are encoded as 768 dimensional vectors by CLIP ViT L/14 Radford et al. (2021) and
used as the two modality inputs. DY, Bili Dance, Flickr30k, and SemArt are reserved for adaptation
and evaluation. GraphBind uses a 768 dimensional shared space, two topology GNN layers, a 32
dimensional topology input, eight attention heads, and an eight token topology prompt. Independent
absence uses a 10% missing probability for each modality. Neighborhood correlated absence masks
one hop neighborhoods of seed nodes with a 3% seed ratio. Property dependent absence scales the
missing probability by node properties and sets the average missing ratio to 10% Wu et al. (2026).
Modalities visible to the encoder are additionally masked at 15% to provide reconstruction targets.
Pretraining Optimization. We optimize all GraphBind parameters with AdamW Loshchilov &
Hutter (2019) for at most 500 epochs using learning rate 1 × 10−4, weight decay 1 × 10−5, gradient
clipping at norm 1.0, and cosine decay to 1×10−6 with 20 warmup epochs. Early stopping is enabled
after epoch 300 with patience 150 and minimum improvement 10−4. The InfoNCE temperature is
0.07 and its chunk size is 512. Reliable neighbor completion uses two layers, top 4 latent consistency
neighbors, and a reliability threshold of 0.2. The pretraining objective combines contrastive and
generative losses with unit weights, decorrelation weight 0.05, latent reconstruction weight 0.3, and
latent consistency weight 0.2. The cross modal reconstruction coefficient is 0.5, and hard negative
mining considers at most 4,096 nodes with top 4 hard negatives.
Discriminative Fine Tuning. For node classification, we use a 0.6/0.2/0.2 train/validation/test split
generated by a seeded random permutation. We train for 100 epochs with AdamW Loshchilov &
Hutter (2019) using learning rate 10−3 and weight decay 10−5, and stop after 30 epochs without
validation improvement. The topology prompt has dropout 0.5. The text classifier is selected with
fixed fusion weight 1.0. Few shot node classification uses K ∈{3, 5} labeled nodes per class, 10
episodes, and the same prompt interface. For link prediction, we train for 200 epochs with learning
rate 10−3, weight decay 10−5, and patience 50. We use 0.1/0.2 validation test ratios, one training
negative and 1,000 evaluation negatives per positive edge, edge and score batches of 2,048, two
hop neighbor fanouts of 15 and 15, and a three layer MLP predictor with hidden size 256 and
dropout 0.02. The encoder learning rate scale is 0.1, and the gradient clipping threshold is 1.0. Our
implementation computes Hits@1, Hits@3, and Hits@10; we report MRR and Hits@3.
Generative Fine Tuning. For G2Text, we follow the OpenMAG task formulation Wan et al. (2026).
All methods use OPT-125M Zhang et al. (2022) with LoRA Hu et al. (2022) of rank 64 and scaling
128 applied to q proj and v proj. GraphBind constructs graph prefixes with the G2M-Adapter,
while the compared baselines use a shared prefix projection module. We train for 15 epochs with
batch size 4 per device, learning rate 10−4, weight decay 10−2, AdamW Loshchilov & Hutter (2019)
parameters (β1, β2) = (0.9, 0.95), gradient clipping 1.0, maximum input and output lengths 96 and
64, and beam search with four beams with maximum generation length 64. The graph backend has
20


hidden size 768, two layers, eight heads, dropout 0.1, and at most eight neighbor slots. For G2Image,
we follow the OpenMAG G2Image protocol Wan et al. (2026) and provide graph tokens to Stable
Diffusion v1.5 Rombach et al. (2022). We train at resolution 256 × 256 for 10 epochs with batch
size 16, learning rate 5×10−5, weight decay 10−2, gradient clipping 1.0, and graph context dropout
0.1. Inference uses 50 diffusion steps, guidance scale 7.5, graph context, and up to 4,277 evaluation
samples. All methods finetune the Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net while keeping the VAE and text
encoder frozen. Generated images are evaluated with CLIP ViT-L/14 and DINOv2-large Oquab
et al. (2024).
Baseline Configuration. The eleven baselines comprise five MAG models given by MMGCN,
MGAT, LGMRec, TMTE, and RoleMAG, four GFMs given by GIT, GFT, OpenGraph, and Rie-
mannGFM, and two MGFMs given by UniGraph2 and PLANET. For the MAG models, hidden
dimensions are 128 for MMGCN, MGAT, and LGMRec and 256 for RoleMAG, with their standard
layer counts and dropout values. TMTE uses hidden size 64, ten evolution iterations, four similarity
perspectives, graph skip coefficient 0.8, and topology update ratio 0.1. RoleMAG uses three shared
and backbone layers, modality weights λv = λt = 0.5, and top 16 complementary neighbors. GIT
and GFT use two layer GraphSAGE backbones with hidden size 256. GFT additionally uses a 256
entry codebook. OpenGraph uses a two layer GNN and a two layer graph Transformer with 256
dimensional tokens and 64 anchors. RiemannGFM uses 128 dimensional hidden states and 64 di-
mensional embeddings. UniGraph2 uses a 768 dimensional three layer encoder with eight experts
and top 2 routing. PLANET uses 768 dimensional four layer Transformer blocks, five experts with
top 2 routing, a 20,480 entry codebook, and 10 dimensional Laplacian positional encodings. Pre-
training checkpoints and task specific optimization settings are selected consistently across methods.
