ABSTRACT 

Low-light UAV-based RGB-infrared oriented small-vehicle de-tection is important for nighttime traffic monitoring, emergency response, and urban inspection. Illumination variations, headlight glare, local shadows, and thermal-response degradation cause spa-tially varying modality reliability, while the small visual extent of vehicles further weakens boundaries, orientation cues, and thermal responses. Accordingly, selecting trustworthy observations based on local modality reliability while further exploiting complemen-tary discriminative information in regions with ambiguous modality preference is key to constructing effective multimodal representa-tions. Based on this insight, we propose ReDiffNet, a reliability-conditioned differential representation network in which modality reliability guides both evidence selection and complementary recov-ery. Specifically, degradation-aware reliability learning estimates relative spatial reliability, uncertainty-guided differential recovery exploits cross-modal differences to recover complementary cues in ambiguous regions, and reliability-conditioned reconstruction inte-grates retained and recovered evidence into a unified representation. ReDiffNet achieves 85.3% and 73.9% mAP 50 on DroneVehicle and VEDAI, respectively, supporting its effectiveness. 

Index Terms — low-light vehicle detection, UAV imagery, RGB-infrared fusion, oriented object detection, reliability-guided fusion 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Reliable low-light UAV vehicle detection is essential for nighttime traffic monitoring, emergency response, and urban inspection. In this setting, a detector must localize small vehicles with arbitrary orien-tations from a top-down view, where targets occupy few pixels and their boundaries are easily confused with road markings, shadows, and background clutter [1, 2, 3]. RGB-infrared (RGB-IR) imaging is attractive because RGB supplies texture and geometric boundaries, whereas infrared preserves thermal contrast when visible illumina-tion is weak [4, 5, 6]. The central challenge lies in determining which modality pro-vides more reliable evidence at each spatial location. Headlight glare, underexposure, cast shadows, weak thermal contrast, and im-perfect local registration affect RGB and infrared differently [4, 5, 7]. Thus, one modality may be more informative at a vehicle bound-ary while the other provides stronger evidence in a dark interior re-gion. Existing RGB-IR detectors mainly emphasize feature align-ment, attention-based interaction, or fixed fusion after feature ex-traction [4, 5, 6, 8]. These advances motivate an explicit spatial re-

We address this issue by casting RGB-IR fusion as reliability-conditioned evidence reconstruction. The proposed ReDiffNet first estimates a spatial field of relative modality reliability using Degradation-Aware Reliability Learning (DARL). It then converts similarity between the two reliability responses into a reliability-ambiguity cue that modulates Uncertainty-Guided Differential Re-covery (UGDR), emphasizing complementary cross-modal differ-ences in regions with ambiguous modality preference. Finally, Reliability-Conditioned Reconstruction (RCR) retains trusted ob-servations and injects the recovered cue through a gated residual update. This trust-recover-reconstruct sequence adaptively routes RGB and infrared evidence while preserving the local structure and thermal saliency required for oriented small-vehicle detection. Experiments on the public DroneVehicle benchmark support the effectiveness of this design. ReDiffNet reaches 85.3% mAP 50 , 4.4 points above the highest reported comparison listed in Table 1. Eval-uation on VEDAI provides additional evidence on a second RGB– infrared aerial dataset, while the component-removal study shows measurable contributions from DARL, UGDR, and RCR under a shared training and evaluation protocol. The contributions of this work are as follows: • We formulate low-light RGB-IR fusion from the perspective of spatially varying relative modality reliability and introduce reliability-conditioned evidence reconstruction for organizing heterogeneous multimodal observations. • We propose ReDiffNet, which couples DARL, UGDR, and RCR in a trust-recover-reconstruct process that preserves in-formative observations and recovers complementary cross-modal evidence in regions with ambiguous modality prefer-ence. • ReDiffNet achieves 85.3% mAP 50 on DroneVehicle and 73.9% on VEDAI, while component-wise studies provide supporting evidence for the proposed design.

To enhance the reliability and complementarity of multimodal rep-resentations under low-light conditions, we propose ReDiffNet, as illustrated in Fig. 1. ReDiffNet comprises three sequentially coupled components: Degradation-Aware Reliability Learning, Uncertainty-Guided Differential Recovery, and Reliability-Conditioned Recon-struction. 2.2. Degradation-Aware Reliability Learning

2.3. Uncertainty-Guided Differential Recovery 

Small-vehicle boundaries, orientation cues, and thermal responses can be attenuated at different spatial scales. We therefore introduce uncertainty-guided differential cue recovery to enhance comple-mentary information according to the ambiguity of relative modality preference. Specifically, average pooling is used as a lightweight spatial low-pass approximation to obtain smoothed modality context. Subtract-ing the smoothed RGB representation from the original RGB feature yields a local-detail residual that emphasizes boundaries and orien-tation cues. In parallel, the absolute difference between RGB and infrared features is processed by two depthwise convolutions with different receptive fields and then modulated by the ambiguity of the reliability estimates:

3. EXPERIMENTS 3.1. Experimental Settings Datasets. We evaluate ReDiffNet on the public DroneVehicle [4] and VEDAI [9] RGB–infrared benchmarks following their official splits and protocols. DroneVehicle contains registered UAV image pairs annotated for five oriented vehicle classes: car, freight car, truck, bus, and van. The 840 ×712 paired images are cropped by 100 pixels along each border to obtain aligned 640 ×512 inputs, concate-nated in the channel order [R,G,B,IR]. Infrared oriented annotations serve as ground truth. The training and validation subsets contain 17,990 and 1,469 pairs, respectively. Main benchmark results are reported on the official test set. VEDAI provides paired visible and infrared aerial images from suburban and rural scenes. Results are reported on its official test set following the prescribed protocol. 

Evaluation Metrics. Following previous DroneVehicle meth-ods [4, 6, 8], we use oriented mAP 50 as the primary evaluation metric, where AP is computed at an IoU threshold of 0.5 and aver-aged over all object categories. For controlled ablation experiments, we additionally report Precision, Recall, F1, and mAP 50:95 , where 

mAP 50:95 averages AP over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 with a step of 0.05. 

Implementation Details. ReDiffNet is implemented in PyTorch and integrated into an Ultralytics-based oriented detection framework. The reconstructed representation is supplied to the shared multi-scale backbone and oriented detection head, and all reported variants use the same detection scaffold. Training is conducted on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU for 30 epochs at 640 ×640 resolu-tion with a batch size of 24. We use AdamW with an initial learning rate of 0.001111, weight decay of 5 × 10 −4, a three-epoch warm-up, cosine learning-rate decay, and random seed 0. Data augmenta-tion comprises translation, scale, and horizontal flipping to preserve paired-image correspondence. For reliability supervision, a training pair uses the original paired observations with probability 0.70, while RGB or infrared degrada-tion is sampled with probability 0.15 each. The auxiliary reliability loss has weight λrel = 0 .05 . Main benchmark evaluation uses the original test images with standard single-pass inference.

4. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose ReDiffNet, a reliability-conditioned dif-ferential representation network for low-light UAV-based RGB– infrared oriented vehicle detection. ReDiffNet follows a trust-recover-reconstruct process that combines relative reliability learn-ing, ambiguity-guided differential recovery, and reliability-conditioned reconstruction. Experiments on DroneVehicle and VEDAI support the effectiveness of the proposed framework. Future work will explore its extension to broader multimodal perception tasks. Acknowledgment
